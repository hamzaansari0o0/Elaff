Frozen mango pulp wholesale means buying ripe mango puree or pieces, frozen and packed in bulk, for juice makers, bakeries, dairies and food manufacturers. Specify the variety, sweetness, packing and freezing method, and ask for a sample, before you place a container order.

## Pulp, puree or IQF pieces?

Buyers use these words loosely, so confirm exactly which product you are quoted.

- **Mango pulp or puree:** ripe mangoes are peeled, the stone is removed and the flesh is milled into a smooth pulp, then frozen. It blends straight into drinks, ice cream, yoghurt, sauces and bakery fillings.
- **IQF mango pieces:** cubes, slices or chunks that are frozen individually, so they stay loose in the bag. They suit smoothies, toppings, fruit salads and retail frozen-fruit packs.

Pulp is not the same as aseptic or canned mango puree, which is cooked, sealed and stored without freezing. Frozen pulp keeps a fresher, fruitier flavour but must stay frozen from the factory to your kitchen. ## Variety and flavour

The variety changes the taste. Buyers often ask for Alphonso, which is known for its sweetness and aroma, or for Totapuri, which is tangier and common in processing. Ask what is available now, because mango is a seasonal crop and the variety can change from one shipment to the next.

## Specifications to ask for

Put these in writing before you order, so every shipment matches your sample:

- **Variety and origin** of the fruit.
- **Brix:** the sugar level of the pulp, measured in degrees Brix. A higher figure means a sweeter, more concentrated pulp.
- **Acidity:** it balances the sweetness and affects how the pulp tastes in a drink.
- **Additives:** pure pulp with nothing added, or sweetened or preserved pulp.
- **Texture and colour:** smooth or with fibre, and the colour range you accept.
- **Packing:** the size of each bag or pail, the net weight per carton, and your own label if you need one.
- **Shelf life** on arrival and the storage temperature, which is -18 °C or colder for frozen pulp.
- **Test reports:** a certificate of analysis for each batch, plus halal and HACCP certificates if your market needs them.

## Where frozen mango pulp is used

- Fruit juices, nectars and smoothie bars.
- Ice cream, sorbet, yoghurt and other dairy desserts.
- Bakery fillings, cakes, mousses and sauces.
- Ready-to-drink beverages made by contract bottlers.
- Cocktail and drink mixers for hotels and restaurants.

## Season and supply

Mango is a seasonal crop, and most pulp is made during the harvest, then frozen and stored for sale through the year. That makes the crop year important. Ask which season the pulp was made in, because sweetness and colour can differ slightly between years. Ask too how much stock is available, and whether the supplier can keep the same specification for repeat orders, so your product tastes the same each time you reorder.

## Quality checks when the pulp arrives

Frozen pulp should look and smell like ripe mango. Check these points on the sample and on every delivery:

- **Colour:** an even golden yellow to orange. Brown or grey patches suggest oxidation or poor handling.
- **Smell and taste:** fresh and fruity. A sour, fermented or alcoholic note means the fruit was overripe or the pulp warmed up.
- **Texture:** smooth, or with fibre only if the specification allows it. Black specks can be skin or stone fragments.
- **Packing:** bags or pails that are intact, sealed and not swollen.
- **Ice:** large ice crystals or a block that has melted and refrozen point to a break in the cold chain.

If your product is sold under your own brand, run a taste test against your last approved batch, because natural fruit varies from season to season.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the difference between mango pulp and IQF mango?
Mango pulp is smooth puree made from milled ripe fruit and frozen. IQF mango is cut into cubes, slices or chunks that are frozen individually and stay loose. Pulp suits drinks and desserts, and pieces suit toppings and retail packs.

### What does Brix mean in mango pulp?
Brix measures the dissolved sugar in the pulp, in degrees. A higher Brix means a sweeter, more concentrated pulp, so ask for the range in the specification.

### How should frozen mango pulp be stored?
Keep it at -18 °C or colder until you use it. Thaw only what you need and do not refreeze it.
