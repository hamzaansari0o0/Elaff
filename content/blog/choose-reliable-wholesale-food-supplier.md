A reliable wholesale food supplier is a real, traceable company that gives you a written specification, current certificates, honest samples and clear terms. Verify its identity, test the product, protect your payment and begin with a small trial order before you buy by the container.

## Why checking matters

Food orders are large, shipped far away and hard to reverse. A wrong product, an expired certificate or a supplier who disappears after the deposit can cost more than the goods themselves. A short checklist at the start prevents most of these problems.

## The checklist

| Area | What to check |
|---|---|
| Identity | Registered company name and number, a real address, a company email, a working phone number |
| Product | A written specification: size, grade, packing, shelf life and storage temperature |
| Samples | A sample that matches the specification, and a test report if you need one |
| Compliance | Current halal, HACCP or other certificates, with scope and dates |
| Terms | The trade term, the destination port, the lead time and who pays each cost |
| Payment | A written payment plan with a deposit and a balance against documents |
| Documents | The list of papers that will come with each shipment |

## Check identity first

Ask for the company's registered name and number, and look them up in the public register of its country if one exists. Check that the address, the phone number and the website all point to the same business. Arrange a video call, and ask to see the warehouse, the products or the loading, if that is possible. A supplier that has nothing to hide will usually agree.

## Questions to ask on the first call

A direct, specific answer tells you more than a brochure. Ask:

1. Which plant produces this product, and where is it?
2. Who holds the certificates, the supplier or the producer?
3. What are the lead time and the minimum order for my quantity?
4. How is the product packed, and how many cartons fit on a pallet and in a container?
5. What will you do if the goods do not match the specification?
6. Which documents will you send with each shipment?

Note the answers and compare them with the written quote. Anything that changes between the call and the quote needs an explanation.

## Get everything in writing

Ask for a written specification for each product, and put it in the purchase order. Include the size or grade, the packing, the net weight per carton, the shelf life on arrival, the storage temperature and the labelling. If a dispute starts later, the written specification is what counts.

## Test with a sample

Ask for a sample before the first order, and test it in your own kitchen, store or lab. Check the quality, the packing and the labels, and compare the sample with the specification. Keep the sample. When the first shipment arrives, compare the goods with it. Sample terms differ between suppliers, so ask who pays for the sample and for its shipping.

## Check the certificates

Ask for current certificates that cover the product and the plant, and confirm them with the issuer. Our guide to [halal and HACCP certification](/blog/halal-haccp-certification-importers) explains what to look for.

## Protect your payment

- Do not pay the full amount in advance to a supplier you have not verified.
- Agree a deposit with the balance due against shipping documents, such as a copy of the bill of lading.
- For large orders, ask your bank about a letter of credit, which pays the supplier only when the agreed documents are presented.
- Pay only to a bank account in the company's name, and be suspicious of last-minute changes to the account details.

## Consider independent inspection

For large orders, hire an independent inspection company to check the goods before they are loaded. It can verify quantity, packing, labels and quality against your specification, and it gives you a report if you need to make a claim.

## Warning signs

- Prices far below the market.
- Pressure to pay quickly or to skip the sample.
- Refusal of a video call or a sample.
- A certificate that does not match the product or the company.
- Details that change from one message to the next.
- No company email, only a chat app.

## Start with a trial order

Place a small first order, even if it costs a little more per carton. It tests the supplier's quality, paperwork and communication before you risk a container. Our guide to [starting small](/blog/import-food-from-one-pallet) shows how.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I verify a supplier's business registration?
Ask for the company's registered name and number, and look them up in the public register of its country. Check that the address and contact details match the company's website and documents.

### Should I pay a deposit to a new supplier?
A deposit is normal, but do not pay the full amount in advance. Agree a deposit with the balance due against shipping documents, and pay only to an account in the company's name.

### Are samples free?
It depends on the supplier and the product. Ask before you request one, including who pays for shipping.

### What is a pre-shipment inspection?
An independent company checks the goods before loading, comparing quantity, packing, labels and quality with your specification, and issues a report.

### What are the biggest warning signs?
Prices far below the market, pressure to pay quickly, refusal of a sample or video call, and certificates that do not match the product or the company.
